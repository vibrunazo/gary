Round one, 2026-11-22

## Is a major refocus necessary?

**Refocus what gets built and measured next, but don't approve a major architectural rewrite.** The current direction can keep improving the opponent. More bug fixes and more training on the same targets will not, by themselves, deliver a requested replay drill, a calibrated skill slider, or a record of why Gary changed plans. Build one small, authoritative plan-and-trace path within the current agent. Expand it only where a simpler change fails a product test.

- **What the goals require (repo read):** G1 asks for human-like play at a target level, not maximum wins; G2 and G6 constrain the interface and experiment design; G3 requires reliable steering from text or replay; G4 requires multiple skill bands; G5 requires an honest account of intentions, beliefs, alternatives and constraints, not full neural transparency. All matchups remain a product goal. The original design already specified a Plan Supervisor, a selector, and separate drill and adaptive modes. This is chiefly a change in delivery order, not a reversal of the design. Sources: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:38–64, 188–197, 475–492, 518–524`.
- **Why the existing direction is worth keeping (repo read):** It already moved from aggregate resemblance to situational diagnosis, execution checks, upstream army positioning and watched games, and parked RL pending a clean pipe and usable reward (`TODO.md:7–46, 136–145`; `AGENTS.md:45–91`). The ledger has real progress without a new hierarchy: the gas head brought style-1 Factory-by-8 to 95% vs. 73% in its paired baseline and 88% in pros, and improved style 2's natural timing (`docs/PROGRESS.md:1516–1533`). Fixing builder contention materially reduced the bank and narrowed the measured Terran dev deficit; the residual deficit was not localized to combat or to plan selection (`docs/PROGRESS.md:2959–2979`). Correcting TvZ labels restored siege use; the later fix for fight-time army unsieges addressed an actual shared boundary bug (`TODO.md:63–74, 94–103`). Those are evidence against an architectural dead end, not evidence that enough patches will eventually make a pro-strength opponent.
- **Why change the near-term priorities (code and ledger read):** The dev manifest rolls one style at construction unless told one, and macro feeds that style plus observed/decision history to its encoder (`gary/agent/manifests.py:1148, 1393–1397, 1659–1664, 1707–1726`; `gary/agent/macro.py:154`; `gary/policy/macro.py:293–306, 481–497`). There is no equivalent here of a replay-specific milestone contract whose progress, detours and failures can be queried. The decision logger already distinguishes choices, alternatives, constraints and orders, but only for the events it actually receives (`gary/decision_log.py:94–145`). The November checkpoint found roughly 25 locally judged changes with neither human play nor a playing-build comparison in that stretch; watching the real client exposed both a missing siege behavior and failures specific to live execution (`docs/PROGRESS.md:1796–1801, 1968–1990`; `TODO.md:7–16`). A historical classifier AUC of 0.99+ is evidence of distinguishability, not a measure of sparring utility or proof of a current ceiling (`docs/PROGRESS.md:108–117`).
- **My inference:** Better imitation, history-aware policies and execution fixes remain plausible routes to G1. G3 and G5 need persistent, inspectable commitments that actually affect decisions; they don't require every scorer, target selector or click policy to be symbolic. For G4, calibrate hands and decision quality separately; there are already profile definitions, but the fit was partial (`gary/interface.py:56–85`; `docs/PROGRESS.md:29–38`). Success would be a repeatable opening drill, an honest explanation of one adaptive deviation, and a watched game that remains playable under pressure. None requires full-game RL to be solved first.

**Recommendation (inference):** Replace Phase 0 and most of Phase 1 with a switchable provider over the present macro and executor. Start with one named TvZ Terran drill, an explicit replay/card ID, a strict mode and one adaptive branch. Compare the pinned current style baseline, a milestone supervisor, and—if the supervisor is too brittle—a learned macro provider conditioned on the next milestones and recent history. Keep a log-only variant as a diagnostic, not as proof of adherence. Add a constrained text-to-card lookup with ambiguity surfaced; don't put free-form interpretation on the execution path. Preserve the cross-race smoke/pipe matrix and try the same contract in one different matchup before calling it race-neutral. Keep `gt1` as a rollback, not a reason to freeze shared bug fixes. Don't delete providers or generalize every contract before the slice passes.

Make the card a partial order of prerequisites and resource/producer reservations, with timing bands and mandatory versus optional milestones. A replay's exact chronology is evidence, not a feasibility guarantee under a different spawn, harvest rate or attack. One macro scheduler must own resource commitments, including gas staffing and construction capacity; a high-priority side queue beside MacroNet would recreate the conflict. Log the first cause of a miss (infeasible, starved of attention, deliberately deferred, or execution failure); don't label an infeasible deviation as policy drift. In adaptive mode try a bounded repair of the unfinished suffix before adding a large directive DAG, soft tech weights or evolutionary search.

### 2. Prove persistence and ownership separately before replacing the tactical stack

RFC §§2.1–2.3, 3 Solutions 1/3/4, 4.2–4.3 Phase 2, 5.3, 6.1 H2/H3. **Code read:** leases are taken after ordering and filter strictly higher priority, so equal-priority sources can supersede one another; they are not exclusive reservations (`gary/agent/intents.py:19–30, 48–86`). The dev `unscripted` switch removes selected army/macro rules, not the fight rule lists (`gary/agent/manifests.py:1090–1096, 1393–1397, 1821–1833`; `AGENTS.md:245–271`).

**Ledger read:** The 90 s whole-army attack-vs-hold contrast is a sensitivity pilot (77 moments), not evidence that a model can predict which option to choose. The cited script was removed, as `docs/SELF_PLAY.md:485–493` explicitly notes. The stronger test already tried a locked engage-vs-move contrast: held-out classification was at chance; on the later pairs a constant engage baseline matched or nearly matched the learned scorers by value, and raising engage odds did not measurably improve whole games (`docs/PROGRESS.md:172–194, 1151–1174`). Even the calm result is not a clean causal diagnosis: the short-order rate 1.43 and the 0.41 lone fraction come from different variants; many short orders went to units already alone (`docs/PROGRESS.md:2228–2234, 2259–2274`). The retreat audit reports 37% **another retreat** in TvZ and 27–40% reposition, close in or fire, not a measured 37–40% cancellation rate (`docs/PROGRESS.md:2404–2412`).

**Recommendation (inference):** Start with a local retreat commitment and a group staging/regroup trial, not nine options. Compare current behavior, a duration/hysteresis-only change, and a persistent option with explicit ownership, holding target and decoder constant as far as possible. Keep the existing spatial model as a target proposal. Fit or check dwell and exits against the pros; allow early interruption on evidence, not a hard 90-second freeze. Measure retreat survival, distance and needless withdrawal, regroup cohesion/readiness, and late arrivals; then measure whole games. Report asked → eligible → scheduled → actual selection → observed execution → transition, with units and age, not just emitted orders.

Put ownership on versioned option instances, not source priority strings. Define atomic transfers, expiry, split/merge and membership of new arrivals; invalidate stale queued work after a transfer and check the actual selected tags at dispatch. Permit intra-option micro (e.g., fire while disengaging if it still serves the disengage) without giving it a second owner. Worker pulls, garrisons and repair can be named bounded skills; scans consume energy and the shared hands, not a combat squad lease. Log emergency preemption and attention denied. Exclusive units do not give a leaf control of the common camera, the APM budget, or the opponent. The current arbiter traces grants and some macro/fight waits, not all blocked option candidates (`gary/agent/arbiter.py:260–286`, code read). Keep that arbiter in the pilot; only add budget scheduling if actual starvation traces justify it.

### 3. Separate faithful decision explanations from counterfactual game advice

RFC §§1 illustration, 3 Solution 6, 4.3 Phase 1, 5.3, 6.1 H1. **Code read:** `gary/decision_log.py:44–51, 94–145` retains truncated, rounded alternatives, optional fields, and separate order/hands events; this is not an exhaustive, joinable trace. For example, the historical schema sample at `runs/league/A50a/Radeon 1.2/gt1dev+attend_seed4009.records.jsonl:18–26` distinguishes a train command from a later wait on a busy Command Center, but has no decision-to-task ID. It does not verify the RFC's second-Factory or attack anecdotes. Input-group zeroing is a model sensitivity probe (`gary/policy/attribution.py:1–11, 67–89`, code read), not a realized alternative game. `eval/pairs.py:241–318` saves/restores games and copies bots with specific commit/engage perturbations and a sampled repeat check; it is not a generic arbitrary-frame concept editor (code read).

**Recommendation (inference):** Use three distinct answer types. (1) **What happened:** log the actual reason, constraints, eligible and rejected alternatives, plan/option versions, model/checkpoint, random draw, evidence time and IDs linking proposals, grants, orders and observed results. (2) **What would change Gary's decision:** re-run the selector on the exact saved input, changing a valid upstream observation or a feasible set of dependent beliefs; preserve the random draw for a local contrast and report probability/score changes separately. Multiple sufficient reasons can make no single flip decisive; an impossible intervention or a changed die can make a flip meaningless. (3) **What would likely happen in the game:** force eligible alternatives from matched snapshots with pinned downstream policies and common seeds where possible; report the horizon, opponents and uncertainty, not a hindsight story about the human match. No simulated branch, no claim about that branch's outcome.

Explicitly mark the RFC illustration as hypothetical or name and cite the exact run/frame. At `docs/REFOCUS.md:52`, a 4:40 sighting cannot explain a 3:31 Factory; only pre-decision evidence can do that. At `:54`, label enemy gas mined as an estimate unless a legitimate observation supports it. Test unsupported premises and abstentions; require every displayed causal assertion to have matching evidence, not just 80% branch flips. Have players answer a practical why-not or predict a branch from the answer, rather than rating how plausible the prose sounds. Deterministic templates prevent invented prose, not mistaken causal attribution.

**Literature opened this run:** Prashan Madumal, Tim Miller, Liz Sonenberg and Frank Vetere, *Explainable Reinforcement Learning through a Causal Lens* (2020), https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/5631/5487 (paper): contrast actual and alternative causal chains, and test whether readers can predict behavior; their StarCraft II participant study is motivation for a usability test, not proof of coaching transfer. Pang Wei Koh, Thao Nguyen, Yew Siang Tang, Stephen Mussmann, Emma Pierson, Been Kim and Percy Liang, *Concept Bottleneck Models* (2020), https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/koh20a.html (proceedings abstract; §7 no. 10), demonstrates intervention on model concepts in classification; it does not equate intervention accuracy with faithful sequential explanations or environmental causality.

### 4. Start with a fog-aware threat forecast, not a universal strict bottleneck

RFC §§1 G5, 2.2, 3 Solution 2, 4.3 Phases 1/3, 5.2 Q1–Q2. **Code read:** the optional supply-belief model is loaded behind army flags, not a full opening posterior (`gary/agent/manifests.py:1782–1792`; `gary/agent/army.py:816–831`). But the claim that the dev build forms no enemy-plan belief at all needs a qualifier: in TvZ it inherits `answers=True` via v029/gt1; its `unscripted`, `styleroll` and `noalarm` options do not clear that setting. `Answers` stores first sightings and a discrete muta/lurker read, and macro uses it to issue goals. That's a heuristic, not a calibrated posterior, and a useful baseline. Sources: `gary/agent/manifests.py:259–267, 855, 1093–1096, 1148–1149, 1224–1226, 1393–1397, 1772–1774`; `gary/agent/answers.py:61–84, 133–149`; `gary/agent/macro.py:219–228`. **Ledger read:** enemy-supply error was higher in league games than on pros (`docs/PROGRESS.md:38, 59`); that does not establish calibration for the proposed new targets. The roughly 19k modern figure is indexed replays, not a guarantee of clean, usable supervision for every concept (`docs/PROGRESS.md:29–33, 59–62`; `ingest/macro_dataset.py:19–24`).

**Recommendation (inference):** Start with one actionable forecast (e.g., mutalisks arriving at a base within a stated horizon) and compare the incumbent heuristic, a time/map prior, and a small replay-derived Bayesian model or time-to-event predictor. Distinguish last seen, confirmed absent after inspection, and not scouted. Define event, region, time horizon and censoring (including early ends); label outcomes separately from in-the-moment inputs. Build a held-out split by games, players and later replay packs; report proper scores versus baseline, discrimination and reliability by time and scout coverage, and what happens at the decision threshold. Cross-check on Gary's games under its actual scouting and on watched games before wiring it to action. Don't let a marginal multi-label output imply a coherent joint enemy build; keep uncertainty/unknown visible.

Keep an inspectable boundary for high-level plan switches; allow rich spatial information under that boundary for placement and target choice. If testing soft beliefs, freeze the supervised forecaster and compare with a decision rule over calibrated uncertainty or joint hypotheses, not just a larger net that ingests a probability vector. Test plausible concept/evidence edits and within-bin stability, not just aggregate calibration. In H4, hold candidate sets, labels, downstream execution, capacity and training budget comparable; compare the same scorer family with and without the extra inputs before comparing EBM vs. MLP. Otherwise the gap is not a bottleneck tax. A named probability may carry information beyond its semantics even without a raw bypass; direct labels mitigate, not categorically prevent, shortcuts.

**Literature opened this run:** Gabriel Synnaeve and Pierre Bessière, *A Bayesian Model for Plan Recognition in RTS Games applied to StarCraft* (2011), https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3735 (abstract and paper introduction): a replay-trained, noisy-observation build-tree baseline actually evaluated in Brood War; recheck under Gary's fog/attention, rather than treating simulated missing observations as equivalent to purposeful scouting. Quentin Delfosse, Sebastian Sztwiertnia, Mark Rothermel, Wolfgang Stammer and Kristian Kersting, *Interpretable Concept Bottlenecks to Align Reinforcement Learning Agents* (2024), https://arxiv.org/html/2401.05821v4 (paper excerpts; §7 no. 5), motivates relations and testing interventions, not necessity of strict bottlenecks in every sequential controller. Marton Havasi, Sonali Parbhoo and Finale Doshi-Velez, *Addressing Leakage in Concept Bottleneck Models* (2022), https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/944ecf65a46feb578a43abfd5cddd960-Paper-Conference.pdf (paper excerpts; §7 no. 8): soft leakage and an intentionally modeled side channel aren't the same thing. Emanuele Marconato, Stefano Teso, Antonio Vergari and Andrea Passerini, *Not All Neuro-Symbolic Concepts Are Created Equal: Analysis and Mitigation of Reasoning Shortcuts* (2023), https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19951 (abstract; §7 no. 14), treats mitigation as conditional, not a guarantee from supervision alone.

### 5. Count what pros did; estimate what Gary should do separately

RFC §§1 illustration, 2.3, 3 Solution 3, 4.3 Phase 1, 5.2 Q4 and 5.3. **Repo read:** the earlier selector did cut losses from 57 to 30 of 333, but most gains came from holding one scripted Zerg's all-in on Fighting Spirit; the training pool and scoring are documented in `docs/PROGRESS.md:4059–4082`. The architecture itself warns that replay win rates are confounded (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:508–516`).

**Recommendation (inference):** Maintain three explicitly different estimands: frequency of a human response in the observable situation; observed outcome conditional on what humans chose; incremental effect of a runnable Gary branch. The first is a good imitation prior, the second is descriptive, and the third can be tested with paired simulator interventions. Do not put the second in a learner answer as if it were the third. Define branch eligibility at first sighting, use games not frames as independent units, and account for already-built defenses, unobserved sightings and games ending before the response window. Shrink and report support; don't imply that shrinkage repairs selection bias. Best initial scorer: a declared drill default or empirical human frequency with an explicit fallback, not a win optimizer. For adaptive outcomes, randomize only among feasible options in the simulator, log assignment probabilities, pair from the same decision checkpoint with declared continuation, and validate across an opponent panel plus live play. This establishes an effect under those policies, not against humans generally. Sequential changes alter later opportunities; a contextual bandit is reasonable only for a declared local branch with fixed continuation/reward protocol, not an off-the-shelf solution for a whole trajectory.

**Literature opened this run:** Adith Swaminathan and Thorsten Joachims, *Batch Learning from Logged Bandit Feedback through Counterfactual Risk Minimization* (2015), https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume16/swaminathan15a/swaminathan15a.pdf (paper; §7 no. 21). Its use of logging propensities and support does not recover outcomes for choices the pros never made or remove confounding by unrecorded information or player skill. A fitted propensity from those same observational logs doesn't fix either problem.

### 6. Fix the learning ledger and de-risk training in the right order

RFC §§2.3, 3 Solution 4, 4.3 Phase 2, 6.1 H3. **Settled from code:** `rl1` is still in the `gt1dev` option chain, but later `tvzmodes` sets `roles_model=None` and selects the relabelled TvZ data. The loader prefers a pin, otherwise the checkpoint trained on the configured data, so the exact binary is not fixed by the manifest name. Correct the stale ledger entries instead of treating the option string as the active model. Sources: `gary/agent/manifests.py:730, 994–999, 1357–1364, 1393–1397, 1606–1627, 1663–1664, 1821–1826`; compare `docs/PROGRESS.md:20–27, 38–39`. The prior RL improvement should still count as evidence: the historical paired result and its pipe prerequisites are documented (`gary/agent/manifests.py:1233–1235`; `docs/SELF_PLAY.md:520–530`).

**Recommendation (inference):** Don't make RL a release gate or call single-decision learning hopeless. Demonstrate a clean pipeline and usable label semantics first. A fixed trunk and fixed leaves in alternating tests are sensible controls. On learner-visited states, get actual expert or vetted policy corrections for a small targeted set; replay labels from nearby expert states and achieved-goal relabels are different data, not expert answers to novel states. Use disagreement/uncertain examples, allow abstention and hold out whole games. After a leaf update re-evaluate its option's completion, duration and interruptions before comparing trunk versions. Most of the staged learning discipline is already repo policy (`docs/SELF_PLAY.md:473–506, 520–532`; `TODO.md:143–145, 170–171`, repo read).

### 7. Make the acceptance criteria product tests with a credible comparison

RFC §§4.3, 6.1–6.3. **Repo read:** the November `-0.188` is an older supply-graded snapshot with both builds on the builder fix; the critic variants disagreed on magnitude and the November 22 direction already called for a fresh comparison (`docs/PROGRESS.md:2959–2979`; `TODO.md:38–46`). By convention, series with different gg settings are not comparable, and the repo's MDE/promotion rules distinguish fidelity and paired play (`AGENTS.md:45–74`). The interface already has profiles with multiple parameters but a number are estimates (`gary/interface.py:56–85`, code read).

**Recommendation (inference):** Three human games are a discovery checkpoint, not H1 acceptance or a veto overriding a clearly worse paired result in H2. Pin code, real checkpoints, opponents, styles, maps, gg/grading rules and seeds (the loader can choose newer models, `gary/agent/manifests.py:1606–1627`, code read); repeat both baselines on current shared code. Use the existing 18/20 unharassed drill target, a separate suite of held-out disruptions with agreed recovery/deviation behavior, and watched/ideally blinded comparisons of same-drill games. Score success at the drill, coherence, exploitable behavior, and usefulness of paired why/why-not answers; report counts, uncertainty, and abstentions. Pre-register a practically tolerable loss and require the interval to rule out worse than that margin to claim non-inferiority; an interval containing zero or a point loss below the MDE alone is inconclusive. Measure completion without gg as a separate finishing benchmark; use the same gg setting for both sides in any promotion comparison. Schedule a small two-profile test early (same plan, different human input/attention budgets and decision error rates); check ordering in blinded sparring and whether a weaker profile still honors the drill. Keep per-situation and matchup metrics as diagnostics, not a backlog generator or a substitute for that test.

### 8. Defer automated discovery until a demonstrated decision failure needs it

RFC §§3 Solution 5, 4.3 Phase 3, 5.3. **Code read:** v2 is not a universal frozen kNN projection. TvZ Terran uses kNN on opening features; several other matchup/race catalogues use explicit plan labellers and a frequency-based `other` (`taxonomy/label_all.py:39–83, 126–151, 155–174`). `ValueNet` predicts win and multiple future trade targets, not the value of a specific branch under a fixed continuation (`gary/policy/value.py:14–16, 75–108`).

**Recommendation (inference):** Permit unknown or hybrid exemplars without retraining a taxonomy; use simple nearest-exemplar support, coverage and a manual queue of repeatable failure cases before adding phase embeddings. Time bins alone split the same delayed plan across phases; align on milestones and stratify checks by map, era, matchup, scouting and disrupted versus clean play. Test false alarms on rare known builds as well as recall on held-out ones. If auditing a residual, first fit comparable models to the same target/horizon on the same information and cross-fit; residuals can be target mismatch, underfitting, fog leakage, opponent mix or miscalibration. Better prediction of whole-game wins needn't improve any action choice. Promote a concept only if it is observable or testably inferable when needed, improves a named decision on an untouched split with adequate support, and survives an intervention plus a human semantics check. Mine repeated useful blocks from losses as well as wins; otherwise the library can mistake winner-state artifacts for reusable strategy.

**Literature opened this run:** Yichao Liang, Nishanth Kumar, Hao Tang, Adrian Weller, Joshua B. Tenenbaum, Tom Silver, João F. Henriques and Kevin Ellis, *VisualPredicator: Learning Abstract World Models with Neuro-Symbolic Predicates for Robot Planning* (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23156 (abstract; §7 no. 12): invents predicates for abstract planning in simulated robotics; it does not validate the proposed win-residual discovery or promotion rule. Don't claim the work has no RTS-scale precedents for particular subproblems when the build-order and opponent-modelling work above studies the same game.

### 9. Fix the plumbing diagnosis and keep the proposed contracts honest

RFC §§2.4, 4.1–4.2. **Code read:** `ingest/macro_dataset.py:3–24, 34–68` documents a replay-event extractor/serializer, not a second macro encoder. Training already imports and invokes the encoder from `gary.policy.macro`; live vectors use it too (`train/macro.py:41, 217–224`; `gary/policy/macro.py:80–123, 481–497`). The parity test actually compares separately collected inputs and lists intended and known differences (`tests/test_inputs.py:9–15, 40–65, 246–288`). There is a parity problem, but sharing a function that already is shared won't eliminate it. In particular, retrospective acceptance of commands in the replay extractor (`ingest/macro_dataset.py:10–17`) is supervision, not legal live state.

**Recommendation (inference):** Share a versioned causal event-to-feature adapter wherever the replay and live paths can see the same event at the same time; keep retrospective labels separate and retain parity tests. The scorer sketch at `docs/REFOCUS.md:351–354` cannot express its own side-channel ablation or score parameterized candidates with just a list of names; pass immutable candidate parameters and declared context/version, and make the experimental extra-input provider explicit. The tactics sketch at `:356–358` needs current commitment and resource state (or an explicit snapshot-able state owner) to define continuation and reproduce branches. Require latency and outcome instrumentation before promising that a fixed squad count solves scalability. Coarse squad control is aggregation, not the same as lifted first-order inference.

**Literature opened this run:** Rodrigo Castellano Ontiveros, Francesco Giannini, Marco Gori, Giuseppe Marra and Michelangelo Diligenti, *Grounding Methods for Neural-Symbolic AI* (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08216 (abstract; §7 no. 16; author order checked against IJCAI proceedings). Its selective grounding concerns substitutions in logical rules and trades expressiveness against cost. It neither proves a fixed squad count sufficient nor removes the combinatorics of feasible targets, concurrent commitments or candidate generation. Correct the lead author's surname in the RFC as well.
