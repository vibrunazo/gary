Round one, 2026-11-22

## 1. Is a major refocus necessary?

**No — not the refocus this plan proposes. A smaller, targeted addition is necessary for G3, G4 and G5; the current direction is the right way to get G1 and is not "just more bug fixing".**

The plan's diagnosis is aimed at the dev builds, not at the current direction. The direction recorded in `TODO.md` is a measurement loop: judge what a pro would do in Gary's situation (`eval/judge.py`), check every drawn decision against what Gary carried out (`eval/pipe_table.py`), read the records when a round fails (`eval/why.py`), watch one live game a race, and only then run a paired round (`TODO.md:7-46`). Its own root-cause finding is not "the models are marginal behavior cloning"; it is that "most faults found were in the pipe between a model's odds and what Gary carried out" (`TODO.md:14-16`). The plan says the same thing in its own words — the neural outputs "frequently never reached the units or were overwritten mid-execution" (`REFOCUS.md:67`) — and then concludes that the architecture must be replaced. Those are two different conclusions from the same evidence. The repo's conclusion is to fix the pipe; `eval/pipe_table.py` v1 "finds four of the five known faults" (`TODO.md:22-25`), and the current list of fixes came from it (whole-cluster orders, the calm layer, the retreat point, the gg rule).

The current direction is producing measurable movement, not just churn. The Terran's tanks went from 0% sieged in TvZ to 61.3% of their fight moments for a pro's 54.3% (`TODO.md:66-69`); the retreat point was fixed (`retreatfar`, `TODO.md:29-32`); the calm layer's short orders rose from 0.31 to 1.24-1.43 a fighter a minute against the pros' 1.58, and a new fighter's first rest fell to the pros' 8 s (`PROGRESS.md:2262-2266`); army orders went from 22% to one unit to 43% at eight or more, and the army's spread and fight gap moved nearer (`PROGRESS.md:2147-2163`); league games now end at the critic's gg (`PROGRESS.md:2291`). Those are not "more bug fixing" in the dismissive sense; they are the exact class of fault the plan says the architecture is needed to solve.

The repo's own evidence also does not support "the stop-gaps killed RL" as the primary blocker. The first roles run that held was the one where the pipe check passed and the model owned the decision in contact; the run before it had the model competing with `RoleAhead`'s hand rules (`SELF_PLAY.md:479-483`, `:520-526`). The lease code is *preemptive*, not post-posed: `Leases.free()` removes the units a higher-priority source holds before the lower layer decides (`gary/agent/intents.py:54-60`). The failure was that the model was never asked or never carried out, which is a pipe and ownership problem, not a shielding-order problem.

What the current direction genuinely does *not* do is G3, G4 and G5. The architecture itself says *z* is "a build-style cluster, no prompts" (`ARCHITECTURE.md:18`), the belief model is "not used in play by default" (`PROGRESS.md:59`), the strategy selector is "built light" from Gary's own games with no pro statistics and no conditioning on what was scouted (`ARCHITECTURE.md:25`), and P5 explainability is "—" (`PROGRESS.md:40`). More bug fixing and more training will not produce a prompt-to-plan compiler, a belief in play, a plan state, or a narrator. So the answer is split: **no major refocus for G1/G2/G6; a small, gated refocus for G3/G4/G5.** The smallest version is already designed in the repo: wire `belief.py` plus `taxonomy/plans.py` into play (`ARCHITECTURE.md:756-766`), extend `gary/agent/selector.py` with the pro table and the belief context (`ARCHITECTURE.md:475-525`), add the `strategy_compile` slot (`ARCHITECTURE.md:191`, `:383-446`), and add the records and templates from `ARCHITECTURE.md:842-868`. That is a G3/G5 sprint, not a six-solution sandwich, and it does not require replacing the dev builds.

The plan's own Phase 1 is close to that, but it adds a concept bottleneck and a new symbolic trunk before the belief and the prompt compiler are in play, and its acceptance gate is three live games (finding 8). That is the wrong order of risk: the smallest unbuilt pieces are the cheapest to prove and the ones the goals actually name.

### Finding 10. The strict concept bottleneck is the weakest form of the idea; post-hoc CBM, Concept Embedding Models, and the repo's own `ValueNet` residual loop are better defaults, and the plan's H4 is a likely outcome.

- Doc: §3.2, `REFOCUS.md:172-178`; §3.6, `REFOCUS.md:302`; §6.1, `REFOCUS.md:454`.

- Finding: The plan's "strict bottleneck" says the trunk receives only the named concepts and no raw bypass (`REFOCUS.md:172`). The plan's own H4 says this may lose more than 0.12 paired score (`REFOCUS.md:454`); the literature has cheaper fixes: Post-hoc CBM turns any network into a concept bottleneck without the accuracy loss (Yuksekgonul, Wang, Zou, ICLR 2023, arXiv:2205.15480), and Concept Embedding Models reduce the leakage/accuracy trade-off (Espinosa Zarlenga et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2209.09056). The "CBM" literature is about learning concept predictors; it does not justify making the symbolic trunk blind to everything but concepts. Start with the §7.6 loop (table + `ValueNet` residual, leaves free to see raw features) and measure the strict version as the Q1 ablation.

### Finding 18. Better solutions in the literature: plan recognition for the opponent, hierarchical imitation for the options, offline RL for the leaves, a post-hoc CBM for the concepts, and an LLM planner for G3.

- Doc: §3.2 (`REFOCUS.md:167-170`), §3.3 (`REFOCUS.md:203-209`), §3.4 (`REFOCUS.md:243-251`), §3.5 (`REFOCUS.md:267-274`), §3.6 (`REFOCUS.md:299`).
- Source: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:756-766` (§7.5: the opponent model is `P(enemy_z | what I saw)`, not a generic multi-label classifier); `taxonomy/plans.py:1-40` (the Zerg plan library: pool/hatch first, 2/3 hatcheries, spire/lurker/den); `ingest/army_dataset.py:28-36` (the pros' 5 s commitment labels: keep/engage/move), `train/commit.py:1-8` (the commitment as the unit); `train/commit_pairs.py` and `train/value.py` (the offline branch data and the critic); `gary/agent/selector.py:1-20`. Read in source; literature opened (below).
- Finding: The plan's opponent model is a multi-label classifier over named concepts (`REFOCUS.md:167-170`). The architecture already specifies the better-fitting object: a distribution over the enemy's *z* from a plan library (`ARCHITECTURE.md:756-766`), and the repo has the plan library in `taxonomy/plans.py` and the re-simulated games to fit it. The literature's plan-recognition model for StarCraft is Bayesian over a plan library (Synnaeve & Bessière, AIIDE 2011, arXiv:1111.3735); it is more data-efficient and more explainable than a multi-label classifier, and it uses the same "what did I see" inputs. The plan's 8-9 squad options (`REFOCUS.md:203-209`) are hand-designed; Hierarchical Imitation and RL (Le et al., ICML 2018) learns the option boundaries and the option policies from unsegmented demonstrations, which is exactly the "pro replays have no option labels" problem the plan's own §3.4 admits. The plan's "bounded local skill RL" is online (`REFOCUS.md:243-251`); AlphaStar Unplugged (Mathieu et al., 2023) and the offline RL line it benchmarks (CQL/IQL) are the better fit for the repo's existing 92k replays and its "learning from counterfactuals is offline" rule. The plan's strict CBM is the weakest form (Finding 10); the post-hoc CBM and CEM are the better defaults. The plan's LLM is only a narrator (`REFOCUS.md:299`); HIMA (Ahn et al., COLM 2025) and TextStarCraft (Ma et al., NeurIPS 2024) use the LLM as the strategic planner, and the repo's `strategy_compile` slot (`ARCHITECTURE.md:383-446`) is the place for it.
- Fix: Re-order the plan: (1) plan recognition over the existing `taxonomy/plans.py` plus the `selector.py` table, (2) the `StrategyState` and the `strategy_compile` prompt, (3) options learned from the pros' commitments (hierarchical imitation) before hand-named options, (4) offline RL on the existing paired branches, (5) the post-hoc CBM for the concept audit, (6) the templates and the narrator. The plan's "strict CBM first" and "bounded online RL" are the two bets the literature and the repo's own rules argue against.
